# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Primary (core app):** Families researching where to relocate — browsing at home, often evenings, making one of the biggest decisions of their lives. They want reassurance and clarity, not raw data.

**Primary (monetization surfaces):** Estate agents — now a *paying B2B customer*, not just a secondary viewer. They configure their agency branding, buy reports (one-off or subscription), and hand a polished, branded PDF to their own clients. The brand-settings, checkout, and report-generation screens serve this user.

**End viewer of the PDF:** the agent's client (a prospective homebuyer). The report is the agent's premium collateral and must read as the *agency's* work.

## Product Purpose

Boreas turns scattered public location data (climate, wind, sun, hazards, air quality, local context) into a calm, digestible neighbourhood dashboard for any location. It exists to make a high-stakes relocation decision feel understandable.

Monetization: sell **agency-branded PDF location reports**. The dashboard data becomes a white-label leave-behind / listing attachment that estate agents pay for (£9 one-off or ~£25/mo unlimited). Success = agents routinely attach a Boreas-powered report to listings, and the report looks like premium work they produced.

## Brand Personality

Reassuring, grounded, clear. A calm expert who makes complex data feel simple and trustworthy — not a military ops center, not a gimmicky map app.

**Calm base, confident money moments.** The map and dashboard stay warm and unhurried (Airbnb/Wise territory). Where money changes hands — pricing, checkout, "generate report" — the UI gets deliberate confidence: clear value, a strong CTA, visible trust signals. Warmth everywhere; decisiveness where it counts.

## Anti-references

- Military HUD, monospace-everything, cyan/amber neon, sci-fi brackets, noise overlays, sharp edges.
- Checkout that feels sketchy, aggressive, or high-pressure — trust is the product.
- A white-label PDF that looks like a generic AI template or a SaaS dashboard screenshot. It must look like estate-agency collateral.
- Zillow/Redfin are *data-presentation* references, not aesthetic ones — don't copy their density.

## Design Principles

1. **Data should comfort, not overwhelm** — neighbourhood insights as clear, digestible cards, not dense readouts.
2. **The map is the hero** — everything else supports the map, not competes with it.
3. **Warmth over precision** — rounded corners, soft shadows, friendly type. Families, not analysts.
4. **Both modes are first-class** — dark mode isn't an afterthought; light mode isn't the only option.
5. **Agent-presentable** — clean enough that an agent can screen-share or hand over a PDF and look professional.
6. **The white-label disappears** — on a paid report the agency's brand leads; Boreas recedes to a quiet "data by Boreas" footer (or nothing). The agent's confidence in handing it over is the feature.
7. **Confidence where money changes hands** — pricing and checkout are honest, clear, and reassuring; never dark-pattern.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG AA**: body contrast ≥4.5:1 (≥3:1 large), keyboard-navigable forms and checkout, proper labels/focus states, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation. Light and dark are both first-class and both must pass.
