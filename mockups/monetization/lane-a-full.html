Climate

A mild, oceanic climate — a narrow temperature range, no extremes, and exceptional sunshine for Britain.

Monthly temperature · °C Daily range (max–min) Mean
Monthly rainfall · mm  ·  annual ≈ 1,010 mm

What this means for you

This is a climate built for living outdoors. Summers stay comfortable rather than hot — peaking around 20 °C — while winters rarely turn truly cold, with means holding near 8–9 °C and frost an occasional guest rather than a season.

The narrow annual swing keeps gardens green and growing for much of the year, and the long Atlantic light gives St Ives some of the brightest skies in Britain. Expect wetter, breezier spells from late autumn through winter — and unusually gentle, golden conditions the rest of the year.

What this means for you

You will notice the light. Summer evenings stretch late — golden hours that run past nine o'clock — and even midwinter days hold more brightness here than almost anywhere else in Britain. It is the kind of place where the sun shapes the day: long beach evenings in July, and clear, low-angled winter mornings that make the short days feel generous rather than gloomy.

An exposed coast has one quiet advantage

St Ives sits at the end of a peninsula, washed by air that has crossed thousands of miles of open Atlantic before it arrives. There is no industry upwind and little heavy traffic, so the pollutants that trouble inland towns — nitrogen dioxide above all — barely register here.

Particulate levels sit comfortably under World Health Organization short-term guidance, and the overall index of 39 lands firmly in the "Good" band for most of the year.

What this means for you

Day to day, air quality simply is not something you will think about. The sea breeze keeps it fresh, smog is effectively unheard of, and for anyone sensitive to pollution — children, older residents, or those with respiratory conditions — this is about as clean as inhabited Britain gets.

What this means for you

By late summer the sea reaches a swimmable 16–17 °C, and the bay's sheltered eastern beaches — Porthminster and Carbis Bay — stay calm enough for families. Porthmeor, facing the open Atlantic, draws surfers all year.

The same exposure that makes the surf brings powerful winter swells and a six-metre tidal range, so it pays to read the tide tables and treat the off-season ocean with respect.

What this means for you

This is a breezy place, and that is part of its character — clean, moving air and dramatic skies. Homes tucked below the hill trade a little of the view for a good deal of calm.

What this means for you

Day to day, the risk here is very low — earthquakes and wildfire are effectively non-issues, and the steep ground sheds rainfall quickly rather than pooling it. The single thing worth checking is coastal and tidal flood exposure: harbour-front and low-lying properties can face overtopping when a winter Atlantic storm coincides with a large spring tide. Most homes, set higher on the hill, are unaffected — and the harbour's sea defences are built for exactly these conditions. Over the longer term, sea-level rise is worth keeping in mind for harbour-front property specifically.

What this means for you

St Ives rewards living on foot. World-class art, three beaches, and a working harbour sit within a few hundred granite-lined metres of one another, and the branch line from St Erth connects you to the national network without a car. The rhythm is seasonal — lively and full in summer, quiet and local through the winter — so think about which of the two towns you are buying into. For many, that contrast is precisely the appeal.
